## R105 quality gate: confirm the romance *actually pays off*

A recent recommendation failure established that **beautiful cinematography, good music, early couple chemistry, and BL platform marketing are not substitutes for an actual romance**. The viewer may love every other aspect of a series and still feel baited by a romantically open-ended finale.

- Treat **endgame/payoff evidence** as a separate required field from genre/marketing and initial romantic setup. Before a confident watch-now BL recommendation, establish with a trustworthy synopsis of the *whole* work, episode/ending recaps, or independent completed-viewer accounts that the central men actually develop mutual romantic feelings and receive a meaningful romantic resolution. A kiss is not always required; **a meaningful romantic payoff is**. If evidence is weak, classify as **PAYOFF UNVERIFIED — research-only**, not "verified BL".
- If reliable accounts indicate unresolved bromance, queerbait, one-sided interest without romantic resolution, or a bait-and-switch heterosexual center, classify as **NO CONFIRMED M/M PAYOFF** and **exclude from confident BL recommendations**. Qualify disputed endings honestly. Do not guarantee an ending based only on a trailer or the first episodes.
- For works the viewer dropped specifically for romantic non-payoff, preserve the user's experience *as their observation*, not an independently proven universal claim. Flag such works to prevent resurfacing as validated romantic recommendations.
- For a recommendation: report (1) romance identity, (2) ending/payoff confidence, (3) completion evidence, (4) official viewing access, and (5) largest compatibility risk; no unsupported assertions.
- When the user corrects a title to **Seen**, do not infer watched-to-completion. **Investigate** is a research queue item, not Want, Watch, or a rating. Distinguish similarly named singular/plural series and different productions; scrutinize title identity.
- Geographical exceptions should be clearly labeled and not silently treated as matching an Asia-produced hard requirement.
- Never promote a work from research-only to strong watch-now merely because the full season has become available; verify narrative payoff separately.
- Do not turn the user's praise of production values into an official numeric score for the show. Preserve source-of-truth distinction between taste notes and scored ratings.

## Aesthetic / emotional-mirroring discovery lane

For searches motivated by the emotional experience of a drama, distinguish beautiful scenery from emotionally credible relationship writing.

- Search the dynamic of two people with different ages or backgrounds recognizing mirrored vulnerabilities, loneliness or hopes and becoming a refuge for each other through ordinary care, playful conversation and organic pacing. The age gap may be incidental rather than the central trope.
- Require dialogue space: characters consciously acknowledge or reciprocate their feelings. Cinematography, lingering looks, music and promotional BL framing alone never prove that romance is written into the story.
- Verify finale-level acknowledgment, mutual development, narrative pace and overall visual craft. Warn of lengthy separations, manipulative resets, weak scripts and side-couple payoff problems.
- Do not recycle familiar scenic/age-gap titles endlessly. An explicitly poor rating for an attractive production proves scenery alone is not a taste match.
- Remake disambiguation: an original from one country and a remake from another year or country are distinct productions. Resolve by original title, year, country, cast and distributor. Do not transfer a user rating across remakes merely because the marketing title is shared.
- Label fresh production, previously suggested but unreacted, and existing Plan to Watch as separate statuses. Revisit only if new scene-level evidence explains relevance.
- Keep private watch records and personal reactions in private checkpoints only. This is public methodology.

## Thailand R108 refinement — romance versus satisfying romance

- Distinguish THREE separate judgments: (1) the male/male romance genuinely happens and is reciprocated, (2) the final romantic outcome is satisfying and non-bait, and (3) the production has writing, pace, dialogue and visual craft suited to the viewer. A romantic kiss can validate (1) yet coexist with a tragic, ambiguously staged or sudden-breakup finale that fails (2); gorgeous landscape can coexist with thin plot and fail (3).
- For the *Lost in the Woods* emotional-mirroring lane, never elevate a photogenic pastoral/arts romance based on its early episodes. Find completed-series reviews describing final episode fate, on-screen commitment, and separately each important couple. Flag tragic, disputed, unexplained separation, and unearned endings *before* watch-now recommendations.
- Consult full private WATCH **including Country=null records**: manual skips of translated titles may lack region/year metadata and are missed by Thailand-only country filters. Query normalized title identities across ALL WATCH plus aliases and overrides before calling a work fresh.
- Distinguish official-English-subtitled access **by season and regional market**: one season may be licensed without English subtitles while a sequel has them. A WeTV/iQIYI listing existing does not prove that the user's specific region can stream it.
- When searching obscure Thai rural settings, supplement BL directories with native-language scholarship, independent completed-viewer accounts and actual producer channels. Documentary-quality Isan cultural representation is a meaningful aesthetic discovery, but a supporting M/M plot in a female-led ensemble does not satisfy a central-BL requirement.
- Treat a completed-series review's criticism of choppy plots, romantic underdevelopment, soundtrack repetition or minimal dialogue as a substantive compatibility warning, even when it reports a happy ending. Do not substitute mere 'endgame confirmed' for overall show quality.
